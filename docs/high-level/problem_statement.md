The Problem: The Friction of Campus Micro-Economies University students operate in a high-density, high-stress environment with hyper-local needs—printing a thesis at the last minute, holding a seat in a crowded library, or grabbing a specific meal from the cafeteria during a 10-minute break. Current open-market solutions (Grab, Lalamove, TaskRabbit) fail in this microcosm. They are bloated, expensive, blocked by campus security, and inherently lack peer-to-peer trust.

The Solution: Side Quests (A Closed-Network Utility) "Side Quests" is a minimalist, peer-to-peer errand network built exclusively for the university ecosystem. Instead of relying on anonymous third-party riders, the app leverages the latent downtime of the student body itself. It is a highly localized platform where students can broadcast immediate, short-term needs ("quests") to be fulfilled by their peers for a set bounty.

Security Through Institutional Trust We deliberately reject the open-market model to solve the universal problem of peer-to-peer security. Side Quests operates as a closed-loop system authenticated strictly via university .edu accounts. We do not need complex background checks or NBI clearances because the environment acts as the filter; malicious behavior is deterred by immediate academic and social consequence. We are leveraging existing institutional trust to bootstrap platform safety.

Who Will Benefit? The system serves two sides of the student community:

1. The Requester: Students who are bottlenecked by time and need immediate, localized assistance without paying exorbitant open-market delivery fees.
2. The Fulfiller: Students looking to monetize the dead time between their classes (a 30-minute vacant period becomes an opportunity to earn PHP 50 just by picking up someone's documents on their way to the next building).

Ultimately, Side Quests is not just a service app; it is a community-building infrastructure. It gamifies mutual aid, turning the daily friction of student life into a self-sustaining, hyper-local economy.
